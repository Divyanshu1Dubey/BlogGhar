The Great Barrier Reef, stretching 2,300 kilometers along Australia's coast, is the world's largest coral reef system and visible from space. It represents one of Earth's most biodiverse ecosystems.

## Why the Great Barrier Reef Matters

**Biodiversity**: The reef supports 1,500 species of fish, 400 species of coral, 4,000 species of mollusk, and 240 species of birds. Many species are found nowhere else on Earth.

**Economic Value**: The reef contributes $6.4 billion annually to the Australian economy through tourism, fishing, and research.

**Scientific Importance**: Coral reefs are natural laboratories for studying evolution, symbiosis, and adaptation to environmental stress.

**Coastal Protection**: Reefs act as natural breakwaters, reducing wave energy by up to 97% and protecting coastlines from erosion and storm damage.

## Major Threats

**Climate Change**: Rising ocean temperatures cause coral bleaching. The reef has experienced five mass bleaching events since 1998.

**Ocean Acidification**: Increased CO2 absorption lowers ocean pH, making it harder for corals to build their calcium carbonate skeletons.

**Pollution**: Agricultural runoff brings nutrients and pesticides that fuel algae growth and harm coral health.

**Overfishing**: Removes species critical to reef ecosystem balance.

## Conservation Efforts

Australia has invested over $2 billion in reef protection. Scientists are developing heat-resistant coral strains, improving water quality, and expanding no-take zones.

## How You Can Help

Reduce carbon footprint, support sustainable seafood, choose reef-safe sunscreens, avoid single-use plastics, and support reef conservation organizations.
