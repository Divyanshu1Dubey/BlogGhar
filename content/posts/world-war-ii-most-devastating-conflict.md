World War II (1939-1945) was the deadliest conflict in human history, involving more than 30 countries and resulting in 70-85 million deaths. The war reshaped international borders, led to the creation of the United Nations, and began the nuclear age.

## Causes of World War II

The roots of WWII can be traced to the aftermath of World War I. The Treaty of Versailles imposed harsh penalties on Germany, creating resentment and economic hardship. The rise of fascism in Italy and Nazi Germany capitalized on this despair. The failure of the League of Nations and the policy of appeasement by Britain and France allowed Hitler to expand German territory unchecked.

## The War Begins

On September 1, 1939, Germany invaded Poland using Blitzkrieg tactics. Britain and France declared war. By 1940, Germany had conquered most of Western Europe. The Axis Powers (Germany, Italy, Japan) faced the Allies (Britain, Soviet Union, United States, and others).

## The Holocaust

One of the darkest chapters in human history was the systematic murder of six million Jews by Nazi Germany. Millions of others deemed "undesirable" were also killed. The Holocaust demonstrated the horrifying consequences of racial ideology unchecked by moral restraint.

## Major Battles and Turning Points

The Battle of Stalingrad (1942-1943) marked the turning point on the Eastern Front. The Battle of Midway shifted the balance in the Pacific. D-Day (June 6, 1944) opened the second front in Western Europe.

## The Atomic Bomb and End of the War

The United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945. Japan announced its surrender on August 15, formally signing on September 2, 1945.

## The Human Cost

The war caused unprecedented destruction with 70-85 million deaths. Civilian casualties far exceeded military casualties due to strategic bombing, massacres, and genocide.

## The Post-War World

WWII led to the creation of the United Nations, the beginning of the Cold War, accelerated decolonization across Asia and Africa, and established the United States as the world's leading economic power.
