Indian classical dance represents one of the world's most ancient and sophisticated performing art traditions, with roots stretching back over 2,000 years to the Natya Shastra.

## The Eight Classical Dance Forms

**Bharatanatyam** from Tamil Nadu combines fixed upper body movements with bent legs and intricate footwork. Historically performed by temple dancers, it features expressive hand gestures (mudras) and complex rhythmic patterns.

**Kathak** from North India evolved from storytelling traditions of nomadic bards called Kathakars. It features straight legs, intricate spins, and complex footwork accompanied by tabla drums.

**Odissi** from Odisha is known for lyrical, graceful movements inspired by temple sculptures, featuring the distinctive Tribhangi posture.

**Kuchipudi** from Andhra Pradesh combines dance, music, and acting, uniquely incorporating elements of street theatre.

**Kathakali** from Kerala is the most visually spectacular form, featuring elaborate costumes, dramatic makeup, and towering headdresses.

**Mohiniyattam** from Kerala embodies softness and grace with gentle, swaying movements.

**Manipuri** from Manipur is deeply connected to Vaishnavite Hinduism and the worship of Krishna.

**Sattriya** from Assam, officially recognized in 2000, originated in Vaishnavite monasteries.

## The Natya Shastra

The Natya Shastra, written between 200 BC and 200 AD, systematically describes the theory of Rasa (emotional essence), various dance forms, mudras, footwork patterns, and facial expressions.

## Contemporary Relevance

Indian classical dance has evolved significantly. Contemporary choreographers blend classical techniques with modern themes. The global reach has expanded dramatically with universities worldwide offering courses.

Indian classical dance was recognized by UNESCO as an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
