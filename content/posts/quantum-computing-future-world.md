Quantum computing represents a fundamental shift in computation, harnessing the bizarre laws of quantum mechanics to process information in ways impossible for classical computers.

## How Quantum Computing Works

Classical computers process information in bits (0 or 1). Quantum computers use qubits, which can exist in superposition — simultaneously representing both 0 and 1. When multiple qubits are entangled, their states become linked, allowing quantum computers to explore many possibilities simultaneously.

## Key Applications

**Cryptography**: Quantum computers can break current encryption (RSA, ECC) using Shor's algorithm. Post-quantum cryptography is being developed to address this.

**Drug Discovery**: Quantum computers can simulate molecular interactions at quantum scales, potentially reducing drug discovery time from years to months.

**Climate Modeling**: More accurate climate models through better simulation of atmospheric chemistry and complex systems.

**Optimization**: Solving complex optimization problems in logistics, finance, and materials science.

## Current State

IBM, Google, Microsoft, and startups are investing billions. IBM's roadmap targets 100,000+ qubit machines by 2033. Current machines are noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices.

## The Quantum Threat to Bitcoin

Quantum computers pose a theoretical threat to Bitcoin's cryptographic foundation, though practical attacks remain years away.

## Preparing for a Quantum Future

Organizations and governments should begin migrating to quantum-resistant cryptography and developing quantum literacy programs.
